Coach

An adaptive fitness companion for training, nutrition and progress.

Coach helps users know what to do today, adapt their workout to the time they have, and keep training and nutrition in one simple experience.

  • Product Design
  • UX/UI
  • Mobile App
  • 2026

The project

Revisiting an old project.

Coach started as an academic UX/UI project built around workout planning, calorie tracking and progress monitoring.

I revisited the product to simplify its structure, clarify its priorities and rebuild the experience around one idea: fitness plans should adapt to real life, not the other way around.

The problem

Too much product, not enough focus.

The original concept tried to solve training, nutrition, social features, progress tracking and community at the same time.

The redesign focuses Coach on three recurring needs: knowing what to train, keeping nutrition on track and understanding progress.

Product focus

Train. Eat. Progress.

The redesign reduces the experience to three main areas that support the user's everyday fitness routine.

  1. Today

    What should I do today?

  2. Nutrition

    How am I doing today?

  3. Progress

    How is my week going?

Onboarding

A plan built around the user.

Instead of choosing from a library of generic programmes, Coach builds the starting plan from a few practical inputs: experience, goal, availability, training environment and equipment.

The plan is only the starting point.

Coach generates the first training week, but the plan is designed to change with the user's schedule and feedback.

Today

One clear answer: what should I do today?

The home screen prioritises the next workout instead of presenting every metric at once.

The user can immediately see the session, duration, intensity and why Coach selected it.

Workout overview

The workout adapts to the time available.

A 45-minute plan is not useful when the user only has 20. Before starting, the session can be shortened or extended while preserving the training focus.

203245min

Missing the perfect workout should not mean missing the workout entirely.

Active workout

Less interface when attention matters most.

During training, Coach reduces the interface to the information needed for the current exercise: sets, repetitions, load and what comes next.

  • Set02
  • Reps10
  • Weight9 kg
  • ActionSet done

The plan learns from the session.

At the end of the workout, users rate how the session felt. That feedback becomes part of the context used to adjust future training.

Stopping early is part of the flow too: the session is saved and the rest of the week adjusts.

Nutrition

Nutrition without turning Coach into a calorie database.

Nutrition remains a core part of Coach, but the redesign focuses on the information users need most often: daily calories, macros and meals.

Goals follow the training plan as well: training days get more calories and carbs than rest days.

Food logging

Reducing friction in food logging.

Food logging was one of the less intuitive flows in the original usability test. The new experience reduces the number of decisions required to add something quickly.

Search→Food→Quantity→Add

Barcode scanning provides a faster route for packaged food, while recent and saved foods reduce repeated searching.

Progress

A weekly view of effort and consistency.

Progress brings the week together at a glance: calories burned, completed workouts, active time, daily average and training streak.

Design system

Built for focus.

The visual system uses a restrained dark palette with yellow reserved for action, selection and progress.

Nutrition introduces additional colour only where it helps distinguish specific metrics.
