The challenge

A fitness app that doesn't make the user feel bad for not opening it.

The brief was to design a mobile app end to end: research, architecture, system and UI. I picked fitness and nutrition because it's a saturated and noisy space — most apps promise military discipline and talk to the user as if they were 25, with two free hours and a gym at home. None of that is reality.

The research made the obvious obvious: people drop these apps because they remind them of what they're not doing. Coach was built around the opposite idea — fit the user's routine, not impose one. Every screen had to remove friction, not add it. Clear hierarchy, an onboarding that asks before demanding, and a dashboard that puts "what's on today" before "how far you still are".

Research

Product goals.

Before touching a single screen, I defined what Coach had to achieve at business and user level — and where the competition was failing.

Key decisions

Three decisions that organise the whole app.

01

Onboarding that asks, not demands.

The user decides how much time they have, what goals they're chasing and what kind of plan they want. The app adapts to them — not the other way round.

02

Home focused on today.

The first screen shows what's on now, not stacked stats. No charts to remind you of what didn't get done.

03

One primary action per screen.

Every view knows its job. Anything that doesn't feed the next action moves to a second level. Less noise, fewer decisions to make.

Final product

Screens.

Onboarding, dashboard, training plans, nutrition and active session. Same visual language across all, but different priorities depending on what the user expects to see when they open it.
